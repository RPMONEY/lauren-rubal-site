Integrative Medicine

Integrative care, explained.

Integrative medicine is evidence-based medical care that treats the whole person. It uses conventional medicine where it works best, and adds lifestyle, nutrition, and carefully chosen complementary therapies to address what's driving your symptoms.

How integrative care is different.

What does integrative mean?

What is

Integrative Medicine?

“It is medicine that is oriented to healing, to understanding we are more than our physical ailments.”

Dr. Lauren Rubal

Integrative medicine looks at the whole person, not just a diagnosis or symptom. It combines evidence-based conventional medicine with appropriate complementary therapies, while considering the many factors that can influence your health.

Most importantly, it is a partnership. We take the time to understand your history, your goals, and the bigger picture so your care can be truly individualized.

What is

Integrative Gynecology?

“You are a whole person. And so we must address and acknowledge your mental, emotional, spiritual, and physical state.”

Dr. Lauren Rubal

To do so, we can take a comprehensive approach. This includes evaluating hormone balances, microbiomes (made up of trillions of synbiotic bacteria that help us in our daily functions), interconnectedness with our organs, inflammation, and the importance of lifestyle factors.

What is

Integrative Fertility?

“I also monitor your cycles closely with ultrasounds and other methods, such as serial hormone levels.”

Dr. Lauren Rubal

Also known as Restorative Reproductive Medicine, it looks at understanding the reasons behind fertility issues using a detailed evaluation of your hormones, inflammation, nutrient status, microbiomes, gut, and environmental exposures.

A tailored plan is created using supplements, hormone optimization, lifestyle changes, and potentially even surgeries to normalize structure & anatomy.

Common questions

Integrative medicine, answered.

Is integrative medicine evidence-based?

Yes. Integrative physicians rely on the same standards of evidence as any other physician. Complementary therapies are recommended when research supports them for your situation, and left out when it doesn't.

Do I have to stop conventional treatment?

No. Integrative care works alongside conventional medicine. If medication or a procedure is the right choice for you, it remains part of your plan.

Will I still see my other doctors?

Yes. Dr. Rubal's care is specialized, and it complements your primary care physician and any other specialists you see.

Is it only for people trying to conceive?

No. The same approach applies to irregular or painful cycles, PCOS, recurrent miscarriage, and hormone transitions such as postpartum and menopause.
